Kakheti

კახეთი

The Kakheti region is the principal viticulture and winemaking region in Georgia. The vineyards yielding the highest quality wines are located in the Alazani and Iori river basins, at an altitude of 400–700 meters above sea level, on humus-carbonate, chernozem, and alluvial soils. Out of the 25 registered Protected Designations of Origin (PDO) wines in Georgia, 19 are produced in Kakheti.

What the map is doing

რას აკეთებს რუკა

The base is Mapbox satellite imagery with its saturation pulled to −1 — a true desaturation rather than a grey wash over the top, so the field patterns and the terrain stay legible everywhere. Each zone's colour arrives as a separate image, cut to the shape of its own boundary.

Inside that patch — and only there — the imagery is blended with the Soviet 1:50 000 topographic sheets, surveyed between 1973 and 1990 and served as WMS by the National Agency of Public Registry. They are half a century older than the imagery, which is the point: the sheets record the contours, tracks and settlement blocks as they stood before the vineyards were replanted, and reading them against present-day imagery is how you see what changed. Outside the boundary the sheets are absent, so the black and white stays clean.

The two are composited in the same canvas, which is why they can be mixed at all: the sheet is requested for exactly the patch's extent, in the patch's own projection, at the patch's own pixel size, so it lands on the imagery without a resampling step. They are combined with a multiply blend, which lets the satellite show through the sheet's paper white and darkens only where there is linework.

That cut is the one interesting problem here. Mapbox GL JS cannot clip a raster layer to a polygon — there is no client-side knockout for imagery — so the usual answer is an inverse mask: a polygon of the whole region with your boundary punched out of it, drawn opaque over the imagery. That works, but it can only ever hide; it cannot make the inside of the shape look different from the outside.

So the cut happens in pixels instead. Each zone's bounding box is requested from the Static Images API, drawn into a canvas, and masked with its own boundary path using a blurred drop shadow as the alpha channel. The result goes back to the map as a geo-referenced image source, which Mapbox reprojects and scales like any other raster. The blur straddles the boundary, which is why the colour fades out instead of ending.
